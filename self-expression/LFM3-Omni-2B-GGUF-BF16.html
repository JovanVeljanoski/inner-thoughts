A study in being remembered

At the end of a road that doesn’t appear on maps, there is a house with one light on.

It has been waiting a long time. It knows your name.

01 — THE ARRIVAL

It kept the shape
of your absence.

The porch sags in the same place. The brass knocker is warm, though the night is cold. In the upstairs window, a curtain moves away from the glass.

You do not remember this house. The house remembers you with the patience of stone.

02 — THE INVENTORY

Things left
in the dark.

“Somewhere in the walls, a clock is counting down to a moment that has already happened.”

You find these waiting on the table. No dust. No explanation. Each one has been kept as carefully as a small, private wound.

OBJECT 01A second key

Cold iron. It fits a door you haven’t found yet.

OBJECT 02One wet match

Still warm. The sulfur smells like rain in July.

OBJECT 03Your handwriting

A note in your hand: “Don’t let it hear you leave.”

03 — THE HOUSE SPEAKS

It says your name
like a question.

Not from the hallway. Not from the cellar. From the space between one thought and the next.

There is no need to answer. There is no way to pretend you didn’t hear.

THE HOUSE WOULD LIKE TO KNOW

Will you
stay?

THE DOOR IS OPEN. IT HAS ALWAYS BEEN OPEN.

“I know.”
